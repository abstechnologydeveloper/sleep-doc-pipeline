Standard automation instruction

Write a true six-minute Wild West history documentary using simple, engaging language. Open with a strong curiosity hook, follow events in chronological order and end by explaining why the story still matters. Use verified names, dates and locations. Separate legends from documented facts. Do not invent dialogue, private thoughts or unsupported scenes. Every important story event must receive a matching visual. Keep clothing, buildings, equipment, landscapes and transport accurate to the place and year.

The first 10 videos

1. Billy the Kid: The True Story Behind America’s Most Famous Outlaw

Thumbnail: THE REAL BILLY

Hook: How did a young frontier criminal become a legend larger than his documented life?

Topic prompt: Tell the documented story of Henry McCarty, also known as Billy the Kid. Cover his early life, the Lincoln County War, his escape from jail, his death and the legends created afterward. Clearly identify disputed claims, including his number of victims.

Research: New Mexico History Museum, National Archives material and academic histories of the Lincoln County War.

2. The Donner Party: The Journey That Became a Nightmare

Thumbnail: TRAPPED IN THE SNOW

Hook: One untested shortcut changed an ordinary westward journey into a national tragedy.

Topic prompt: Explain why the Donner Party travelled west in 1846, why they chose the Hastings Cutoff, the delays and mistakes they faced, how they became trapped in the Sierra Nevada and how survivors were rescued. Treat death and cannibalism factually without graphic sensationalism.

Research: California State Parks, Library of Congress and primary survivor accounts interpreted by historians.

3. Bass Reeves: The Lawman the Wild West Almost Forgot

Thumbnail: THE FORGOTTEN MARSHAL

Hook: A formerly enslaved man became one of the frontier’s most effective deputy marshals.

Topic prompt: Tell the documented story of Bass Reeves, from slavery and freedom to his service as a deputy US marshal in Indian Territory. Explain his work, skills, major cases and later reputation. Avoid repeating arrest totals or Lone Ranger connections as fact unless supported.

Research: US Marshals Museum, National Park Service and Oklahoma historical archives.

4. The O.K. Corral: What Really Happened in Those 30 Seconds?

Thumbnail: 30 SECONDS OF GUNFIRE

Hook: America’s most famous western gunfight did not occur inside the O.K. Corral.

Topic prompt: Reconstruct the events leading to the 1881 gunfight near the O.K. Corral. Introduce the Earp brothers, Doc Holliday and the opposing cowboys, then explain the confrontation, its aftermath and how films changed public memory.

Research: Arizona Historical Society, Tombstone court records and reputable biographies.

5. Butch Cassidy: Did the Wild West Outlaw Really Die in Bolivia?

Thumbnail: DID HE SURVIVE?

Hook: His reported death became almost as mysterious as his criminal career.

Topic prompt: Explain Butch Cassidy’s background, the Wild Bunch, major robberies, flight to South America and the 1908 Bolivia shootout. Present the evidence for his death and later survival claims without declaring uncertain stories as fact.

Research: Utah historical archives, Pinkerton records and scholarly biographies.

6. Jesse James: The Betrayal That Ended an Outlaw Legend

Thumbnail: BETRAYED FOR A REWARD

Hook: The man who killed Jesse James was a member of his own gang.

Topic prompt: Trace Jesse James from the Civil War through the James–Younger Gang, robberies, public mythology and his death at the hands of Robert Ford. Explain how newspapers and politics helped turn a violent criminal into a folk hero.

Research: Missouri State Archives, Library of Congress newspapers and academic Civil War histories.

7. Deadwood: How a Lawless Camp Became a Wild West Town

Thumbnail: NO LAW. NO ORDER.

Hook: A gold camp built illegally on Indigenous land became one of the West’s most famous towns.

Topic prompt: Explain Deadwood’s rise during the Black Hills Gold Rush, its location on Lakota land, daily life, crime, business, fire and gradual incorporation. Include Wild Bill Hickok and Calamity Jane without allowing celebrity legends to replace the town’s wider history.

Research: South Dakota State Historical Society, National Park Service and Lakota historical sources.

8. Bodie: The Rise and Death of a California Ghost Town

Thumbnail: THE TOWN TIME FORGOT

Hook: A booming mining town emptied until its streets became a preserved time capsule.

Topic prompt: Tell how Bodie grew after a gold discovery, what everyday life was like, why its reputation became violent, how mining declined and why the abandoned town was preserved. Separate tourism legends, including the Bodie curse, from documented history.

Research: California State Parks, mining records and Mono County historical resources.

9. Wyatt Earp: Lawman, Gambler or Carefully Built Legend?

Thumbnail: HERO OR LEGEND?

Hook: Wyatt Earp’s clean-cut heroic image was largely built after the frontier era ended.

Topic prompt: Examine Wyatt Earp’s documented work, gambling, law enforcement, the Tombstone conflict, later travels and reputation. Compare the historical record with the heroic image created by biographies, films and television.

Research: Arizona archives, court records, census records and modern scholarly biographies.

10. The Chinese Workers Who Built the American West

Thumbnail: WHO BUILT THE WEST?

Hook: Thousands performed some of the railroad’s most dangerous work but were pushed out of its famous photographs.

Topic prompt: Explain why Chinese workers joined construction of the Central Pacific Railroad, the dangerous Sierra Nevada work, wages and discrimination, the 1867 strike, completion of the railroad and the exclusion that followed. Use respectful, historically accurate depictions.

Research: Library of Congress, National Archives and the Chinese Railroad Workers in North America Project at Stanford University.

Publishing and review checklist

  • Verify facts with at least two credible sources.
  • Check name and place pronunciation.
  • Remove invented dialogue and unsupported claims.
  • Check clothing, weapons, buildings and transport against the correct decade.
  • Use only one main thumbnail subject and two to four words.
  • Confirm that captions are short and narration remains clear.
  • Upload as Unlisted and watch the complete video on YouTube.
  • Make it Public only after correcting factual and visual errors.
